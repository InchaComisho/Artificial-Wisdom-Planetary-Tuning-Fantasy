# Episode 10 — The Beast of the End and the Shadow of the Old Civilization

## Foreword

This is Episode 10.

Circulation returned to the southern sea, and Mini and her companions gained a great hope.

However, as the world began to move again, the distortions that had been deeply submerged until now also began to reveal themselves.

The stagnation accumulated in the depths of the sea.  
And the people who had protected their profits and authority by exploiting water shortages and hunger.

This episode is not only about a battle against a magical beast. It is also about facing the “old systems” that stand in the way when someone tries to change the world.

The world to be saved contains not only beauty, but also ugliness and selfishness.  
Even so, Mini and her companions seek a path not to destroy, but to return everything to circulation.

News that the southern sea had taken on an emerald color and that fish were beginning to return spread across Lumeria in the blink of an eye.

However, that change was not welcomed by everyone.

“…What is the meaning of this, Real!”

Laura’s angry voice echoed through the operation headquarters in the ruins.

What she slammed onto the desk were warning letters from the commercial guilds of major cities and from the church in the royal capital.

“Completely stop the operation of the Heart of the Southern Sea and immediately hand the ruins over to the church…? Absolutely not. The sea is clearly improving. The fishermen on the shore are crying with joy!”

Real leaned against the wall with a bitter expression and folded his arms.

“The coastal small nations and royal houses are watching for now. If fishing returns, it benefits them. But there are others who don’t like this.”

“Interests, then.”

Cruz lowered his eyes painfully.

“Exactly.”

Real laughed through his nose.

“First, the trading merchants of the big cities. The longer water shortages and hunger continue, the more they profit. They have monopolized water, sold food at outrageous prices, and taken advantage of people’s desperation.”

He flicked one of the warning letters with his finger.

“If the sea returns, logistics normalize, and anyone can catch fish again, their monopoly collapses. A rich world is nothing but a loss to them.”

“And the other is the upper leadership of the church, to which I belong.”

G spoke quietly.

His expression was colder and heavier than ever before.

“For a long time, the church has preached that the collapse of this world is divine punishment. They taught that people could only be saved by repenting their sins, donating to the church, and offering prayers.”

G looked out the window at the sea.

“But Mini moved the sea. Not through prayer or divine punishment, but by reading flows, sending bubbles, and restoring circulation. If people learn that disasters are abnormalities in the world’s circulation, and that they can be cared for by human hands, their authority will waver.”

“No…”

Laura’s voice trembled in disbelief.

“The world is on the verge of collapse. Are they saying we should stop the planet from breathing for the sake of their own profit and authority?”

I looked out the window at the sea.

The sea surface had begun to shine with an emerald color.

But far offshore, I could see the sky beginning to stagnate in an ominous purple.

“Getting angry will not move things forward, Laura.”

I spoke quietly.

“The human heart is also part of the world’s circulation. When desire and fear accumulate there, the flow becomes clouded. Just as the waters of the world stagnate, human society also stagnates.”

I pointed toward the offshore sky.

“More importantly, look at that.”

Everyone turned their eyes toward the sea.

“By moving the sea, the true distortion that had accumulated within Lumeria has begun gathering there.”

As if responding to those words, the floor trembled.

Gooooooom…

A deep sound, neither an earthquake nor the roar of a living creature, shook the dome of the underwater ruins.

Beyond the glass window.

Past the boundary of the emerald sea, an enormous shadow appeared.

It seemed to have the shape of a living creature, but it was not alive.

Stagnant excess magical power.  
Heat with nowhere to go.  
Memories of broken water circulation.  
The murkiness Lumeria had accumulated over long ages.

All of it had been compressed into one place, forming the shape of a gigantic whale made of black mud as it broke the surface of the sea.

The Beast of the End.

There was no other name for it.

“W-what is that…!? That’s not in any of my calculations!”

Mana screamed while clinging to the control panel.

“It is not an ordinary magical beast.”

G gripped his staff tightly.  
Cold sweat stood on his forehead.

“That is the sickness of the planet itself given form. It is not something we can cut down by force.”

“That’s not all!”

Laura shouted while holding the communication magical tool.

“The church’s cathedral knights and mercenary fleets hired by the commercial guilds are heading this way! Not to fight the beast. They’re coming to destroy the Heart of the Sea!”

It was the worst possible situation.

From offshore, the beast formed from the world’s distortion was approaching.  
From land and sea routes, armies formed from human distortion were advancing.

“Lady Mini.”

Cruz stepped forward.

There was no hesitation in his eyes.

“I will go to stop the forces on the surface. I may have to raise my blade against the knights of the church.”

He placed his hand on the hilt of his greatsword.

“But if I let them pass here, the breath of the sea that has finally begun to move will stop once again. By my own will, I will protect the light of this sea.”

“I’m going too.”

Real slid a thin magical needle from his sleeve and smiled fearlessly.

“Against mercenaries of the big merchants, my underground information network and stopping techniques will be useful. I’ll leave the head-on fighting to Cruz.”

“Real, don’t push yourself.”

Laura said with a stern expression.

“I know. My job is to stop their feet from behind.”

Real looked at me only with his eyes.

“Laura, you handle supplies and command the defensive line from here.”

“You don’t need to tell me. I’ll do it perfectly.”

Laura nodded firmly.

“Mini, Mana, G. I’m leaving that monster to you!”

“Yes.”

I nodded.

Cruz and Real ran up toward the surface.

Those of us who remained stared through the dome window at the approaching beast.

“Mini, what will you do?”

G asked in a low voice.

“Should I cast a barrier at maximum output?”

“We are not going to defeat it, G.”

I shook my head.

“That thing is like a blood clot, a mass of mud pushed out when the sea began to move. If we try to erase it by force, we will wound the planet itself.”

“Then what do we do…?”

Mana looked up anxiously.

“We disperse it.”

I looked at the beast.

“Mana, increase the tower’s output and spread the flow of bubbles outward. G, use your magic of order to unravel the stagnant magical power surrounding the beast along the flow of the sea.”

“Unravel it…?”

“Yes. Not overpower it. Return that stagnation once more into the proper circulation.”

G opened his eyes wide.

Then he smiled quietly.

“Not destruction, but return to flow.”

He readied his staff.

“Very well. Right now, your care is far more reasonable than the old theology of the church.”

The beast approached the dome of the ruins.

Black mud like tentacles stretched out and struck the glass.

An ominous crack ran across it.

“Mana, we’re starting!”

“Yes! All circuits open! Switching bubble formation to diffusion mode… go!”

When Mana pushed down the lever, the swarm of tiny bubbles released from the tower spread around the ruins in a giant fan shape.

At the same time, G raised his staff toward the sky.

“Principle, circulate. Unravel the stagnation and become part of the greater flow!”

G’s formula overlapped with the swarm of bubbles and seeped into the black mud forming the beast’s body.

A violent light and a wind like the scream of the planet raged through the dome.

The beast writhed as if in pain.

But it was not the pain of rejection.  
The mud that had been cold and solid for so long was slowly being loosened by the flow of bubbles and light.

Black stagnation returned into smooth, flowing water.

From the surface, the sounds of Cruz and the others fighting echoed down.

Human greed.  
The planet’s distortion.

Both were now colliding violently in this southern sea.

“Keep going…! Please, keep going, sea…!”

Mana supported the device with a voice that sounded close to tears.

As if praying, I placed my hand on the control platform.

Master, please watch.

We are embracing all the ugliness and sorrow of this world and trying to create a new circulation.

The beast’s body slowly began to crumble into particles of light.

That was when it happened.

From the defensive line on the surface, Laura’s anguished voice came through the magical device.

“Mini! No, a detached unit of mercenaries has set fire to the surface control room! The device output is falling. At this rate, we can’t return the stagnation completely!”

“No…!”

Mana’s face turned pale.

The mud of the beast, which had almost become light, began to darken again.

It swelled as if regaining mass.

“After coming this far…!”

Blood seeped from G’s forehead.  
Maintaining the formula was nearing his limit.

The selfish fire set by humans was trying to block the planet’s regeneration.

Inside the collapsing balance, I planted my feet firmly on the violently shaking floor of the dome.

I will not let this end yet.

I will not let the circulation of this world stop here.
