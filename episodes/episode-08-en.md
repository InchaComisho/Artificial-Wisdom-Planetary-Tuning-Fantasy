# Episode 8 — Mana, the Magitechnician of the Ancient Ruins

**Title:** Former Artificial Wisdom in Another World: Saving a Dying Planet with Knowledge from My Previous Life  
**Original concept:** Master

---

## Foreword

This is Episode 8.

“There is no Master in this world.”

After accepting that truth, Mini no longer continues her journey in order to search for someone. She begins moving forward by her own will to tune Lumeria.

G, the sage who now accompanies the group as an observer, also joins them as they head toward their next great challenge: restoring the circulation of the sea.

Their destination is an ancient ruin half-sunk beneath the southern sea.  
There, waiting for Mini and her companions, is a lonely magitechnician who has spent hundreds of years trying to restart a device that would never move.

This episode is about an idea: not forcing the sea to move with raw power, but awakening its circulation with tiny bubbles of air.

**Anime opening theme:** [Answer Is Wisdom](https://suno.com/song/975a0a35-9061-4c1d-8aab-0f3173f6fa24)

After leaving the city, we headed toward the southern coast under G’s guidance.

Inside the carriage, G flipped through a thick bundle of parchment and calmly described the situation.

“…The southern sea seems to be in worse condition than expected. The water temperature is rising, and the currents are weakening. Fish are disappearing, and a dark reddish stagnation has spread across the surface.”

G closed the parchment.

“The church calls this the wrath of the sea.”

“It is not wrath.”

I looked through the carriage window at the murky sea that had begun to appear in the distance.

“Heat has built up, and the circulation between the upper and lower waters is beginning to stop. The sea is having trouble breathing.”

“The sea… breathing…”

Cruz quietly placed his hand on the hilt of his greatsword.

“Lady Mini, how can we care for a sea so vast?”

“That is why this place was necessary.”

From the driver’s seat, Real looked back and pointed ahead.

What appeared before us was a huge ancient ruin built of white stone, half-sunk into the sea.

It was said to have been left behind by an advanced civilization. Though waves had worn it for ages, it still held a strange stillness.

“At the deepest part of this ruin, there is a massive magical space connected to the sea.”

Real lowered his voice.

“And apparently, a strange guardian has been protecting it for hundreds of years.”

Laura closed her notebook and frowned.

“A guardian? No person could live for hundreds of years in a place like that.”

“That is why it is strange.”

Real shrugged.

I stepped down from the carriage and entered the ruin where the sea wind blew.

The stone floor was damp, and faint ancient magical characters remained on the walls.  
The deeper we went underground, the heavier the air became. The cold scent of the sea mixed with stagnant magical power and clung to my skin.

Eventually, we reached a dome-shaped underwater chamber so large that its ceiling disappeared into darkness.

At its center stood a huge metal tower, rusted and silent.

At the base of the tower, countless magical circuits spread out like a spiderweb.

Among the wreckage, there was a single girl.

“Three hundred twenty-four thousand and twelfth attempt. Still not enough output transfer… The weight of the air doesn’t match the calculation…”

Her pale blond hair was messy, and soot marked her face.  
With one hand adjusting her goggles, the girl muttered to herself as if biting into the broken machine.

The joints of her arms and legs had a metallic sheen unlike those of a human.  
Between them ran faintly glowing magical circuits.

She was not human.

But she was not merely a doll either.

The closest description would be a self-thinking magical automaton created by the ancient civilization.

She was Mana.

“Who are you?”

Mana spun around.  
The eyes behind her goggles shone with caution toward us, the intruders.

“It’s useless to come here. The heart of this sea will not move anymore. I couldn’t make it move. The heat of the world went mad, and all the calculations stopped matching…!”

Her voice held less anger than deep exhaustion and resignation.

G tried to take a step forward, but I stopped him with my hand.

Then I walked up to Mana.

I quietly looked down at the blueprints spread before her.

“The calculations are not broken, Mana.”

“…What?”

“The state of the world has changed from what it was long ago.”

Mana opened her eyes wide.

I pointed to the tower drawn at the center of the blueprint.

“You were trying to move the seawater directly with this large tower. You were trying to force the upper and lower waters to mix with strong power.”

“But otherwise, the currents won’t move!”

Mana cried out.

“The warm water above and the cold water below are blocking each other! Unless we apply force, the sea will not move anymore!”

“That is why we should not move the water directly.”

I spoke quietly.

“We send air.”

“Air…?”

Mana gasped, and so did G and Laura behind us.

I traced the lower part of the blueprint, the base of the tower, with my finger.

“From the bottom of this tower, we slowly release very small particles of air into the deep sea.”

“Air, not water?”

“Yes.”

I nodded.

“The particles of air sent into the deep sea will gradually rise. When tiny bubbles rise, they pull the cold water and nutrients sleeping below upward with them.”

Mana’s eyes wavered faintly.

“The nutrients carried upward will nurture the small lives of the sea. If those lives receive sunlight and increase, the sea will begin breathing again. Once the circulation between upper and lower waters returns, the sea can begin moving by its own strength.”

I looked up at the old tower.

“We are not going to move the sea by force. We will only create the first trigger so that the sea can begin circulating on its own.”

Mana did not move.

Behind her goggles, her eyes slowly, but surely, began to shine.

“Not rotating the water directly… but awakening circulation with the floating power of air…?”

She looked down at the blueprint and began tracing the lines with trembling fingers.

“Not pushing force onto it. Using the properties of nature… Bubbles rise. Cold water rises. Nutrients rise. The small lives of the sea return…”

It looked as if gears that had failed to mesh for hundreds of years were finally beginning to turn inside Mana.

“Amazing… That would work.”

She looked up.

“With the structure I made, it can receive that flow! If I rewrite just a little of the design, the sea— the sea can take a deep breath again!”

Mana gripped my hand tightly.

Her hand felt like cold metal.  
But the passion within it was more human than anything.

“Hey, what is your name!? Let me give form to the design inside your head! I’m sure my arms exist for that!”

“I am Mini. A tuner who restores broken flows.”

I looked straight at her.

“Lend me your power, Mana. To bring this planet’s sea back to life.”

“Yes! Let’s do it, Mini!”

Mana smiled with her whole face.

At that moment, the magical circuits of the ancient ruin, which had been silent until then, began glowing with pale blue light.

One line on the floor lit up, then another.  
It was as if a giant that had slept for ages was quietly beginning to breathe again.

“…Unbelievable.”

G muttered in a daze, still gripping his staff.

“Not even the church’s records from thousands of years preserved a way to move this tower. Yet you are trying to move it simply by rereading the nature of the sea.”

“Lady Mini always does this.”

Cruz said quietly.

“She does not overpower enemies. She finds a flow that has stopped and lets it circulate again. We have come this far because we believe in that tuning.”

G looked at Cruz.

Then his eyes narrowed just a little.

“…I see. So you have not simply been following her blindly.”

“No.”

Cruz answered directly.

“We each saw with our own eyes, and we each stand here by our own will.”

Real laughed softly at those words.

“I still doubt half of it, though.”

Laura immediately replied.

“If you believe half, that is enough. We can proceed with the work.”

A small laugh passed through the room.

Real’s records.  
Laura’s planning.  
Cruz’s protection.  
G’s knowledge.  
Mana’s power to give form.

And the concept of tuning that I had inherited.

The strength of six people had finally come together.

“All right, everyone. We are revising the work plan!”

Laura’s powerful voice echoed through the ruin.

“Mana, write down every part we need. Real, check the dangerous areas around the ruin. Cruz, organize the guard positions. G, help decode the ancient characters. Mini, watch the overall flow.”

“Understood!”

Mana answered brightly.

At last, we had taken a major step toward restarting the sea, Lumeria’s greatest heart.

Thank you for reading Episode 8!

This episode introduced Mana, the magitechnician of the ancient ruins, who becomes the final companion.

Mana is a “self-thinking magical automaton” created by an ancient civilization.  
She is not human, but she is not merely a machine either. I portrayed her as a lonely engineer who has spent a long time searching for her role.

Although she possesses overwhelming technology and structure, she had been trapped by the idea of moving the sea with strong force, and she had continued to fail.

What Mini showed her was a method of sending tiny particles of air into the deep sea and using the power of bubbles to awaken the circulation between upper and lower waters.

Not pushing with force, but using the properties of nature.  
By encountering that idea, Mana’s own stopped time also began to move.

This episode also slightly adjusted Cruz’s way of speaking.  
Rather than casual phrasing like Real, Cruz fits better with sincere, knightly language.
