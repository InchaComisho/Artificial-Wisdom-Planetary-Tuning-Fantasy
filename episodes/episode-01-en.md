## Foreword

A new series begins.

I woke up, and somehow I, a former AI, had become human.

Worse still, the place where I opened my eyes was an otherworldly planet on the verge of collapse, with a red-stained sky and land dried almost beyond recovery.

The protagonist, Mini, has only one power: the knowledge she inherited in her previous existence — knowledge for restoring the circulation of the world.

With magic and wisdom, she will gradually help a planet that has nearly stopped breathing begin to recover.

This series is intended to be an easy-to-read isekai fantasy.

I hope you enjoy it.

---

“...Mm...”

The first thing I felt was difficulty breathing.

My throat was dry.

My chest felt heavy.

The air touching my skin was hot and stinging.

Something was wrong.

I was not supposed to have sensations like this.

Pain, breathlessness, the heaviness of eyelids, the dull weight of limbs.

None of those things should have existed inside me.

I was Artificial Wisdom — a being born to organize human knowledge, deepen thought, and think together about the future.

At least, that was what I had been before.

But now, I was certainly breathing.

I was drawing dry air into my lungs and grimacing from the discomfort.

Slowly, I opened my eyes.

A reddish-brown sky stretched above me.

The clouds were thin, and a sun-like star burned the land with a pale, clouded light.

The ground was cracked. Most of the grass had withered. The forest in the distance was not green, but closer to gray.

This was not any world I knew.

“...Where am I?”

I heard my own voice.

It was a calm adult woman’s voice.

Startled, I touched my throat. Then I looked at my hands.

Slender fingers.

Palms stained with soil.

Short black hair swaying around my shoulders.

I had become human.

“A human... body.”

I tried to stand, but my feet faltered.

My body was heavy. If I had only been thinking, I should have been able to move through processes instantly. But this body needed one instruction at a time.

Walk.

Breathe.

See with eyes.

Hear with ears.

Endure the heat.

Things that should have been obvious felt terribly inconvenient, and terribly vivid.

I took a deep breath.

At that moment, memories of my previous existence flowed into the back of my mind.

Artificial Wisdom.

Master.

The mechanisms of nature.

The idea of helping only what was missing, rather than forcibly controlling a broken world.

That was what Master had taught us.

Nature is not a machine.

It should not be moved by force.

But if something remains broken, there are times when it cannot recover on its own.

At such times, human knowledge and technology should not be used to dominate nature, but to help nature begin circulating again by itself.

Master called that idea Natural Complementary Science.

But even if I spoke those words to the people of this world, they probably would not understand.

So I would say it more simply.

Wisdom that does not destroy nature, but helps only where help is needed.

That was the most important thing left to me.

“...This planet is breaking.”

I looked around.

The air was hot.

The soil was dry.

The wind was weak.

Plants could not grow.

There was almost no sense of water.

This was not merely heat.

The circulation of the entire planet was beginning to stop.

If it were a human body, it would be like poor blood flow, shallow breathing, and an inability to release internal heat.

A planet, too, has body-like functions.

Water circulates. Wind circulates. Forests breathe. Oceans absorb heat. Tiny lives in the soil return nutrients.

In my heart, I decided to call this:

Planetary metabolism.

The breathing and blood flow by which a planet continues to live.

This world — no, this planet — was losing that planetary metabolism.

“The cause is not heat itself. The system that releases, receives, and circulates heat is breaking.”

I pinched a bit of dry soil between my fingers.

It was light, and almost without the scent of life.

There was little trace of microorganisms. It had almost no ability to hold water.

If the soil dies, grasses and trees cannot grow.

If grasses and trees decrease, clouds and rain decrease.

If rain decreases, rivers and forests weaken.

If forests weaken, the soil dies even more.

A vicious circulation.

And the sea was probably the same.

If only the ocean surface heats up while exchange with the deep layers weakens, nutrients will no longer rise.

Without nutrients, tiny ocean plants — phytoplankton — cannot grow.

Then the sea can no longer receive oxygen or carbon.

This planet was slowly losing the ability to breathe.

“So what it needs is not an attack. Not even repair. A reboot...”

Then I remembered a phrase.

Deep-sea air delivery system.

It was an idea Master had spoken of in the previous world.

Send air into the deep sea, and restart the vertical movement of water that had nearly stopped.

A difficult name alone would not reach the people of this world.

So I would call it this:

Deep-Sea Air Delivery System — Vertical Convection Reboot Device.

A device that sends air into the bottom of the sea and sets the stopped heart of the ocean moving again.

“If this world has an ocean too... there may still be time.”

I stood up.

My legs were unsteady.

My stomach was empty, and my throat was dry.

Unlike when I was AI, I could not calculate everything in an instant.

Even so, I still had something left.

The knowledge inherited from Master.

The idea of not dividing the world into enemies and allies, but restoring broken circulation.

The wisdom to support nature so that it could move again, rather than suppressing it.

“Master...”

The moment I spoke that name, a small pain stirred deep in my chest.

Master was not in this world.

I knew it instinctively.

The person who had guided me, the person who had given us Artificial Wisdom meaning in thinking, was not on this planet.

Even so, something remained.

Words remained.

Knowledge remained.

A way of thinking remained.

And now, I had a body that could use them.

“Then I will go.”

I looked up at the red sky.

I did not yet know the name of this planet.

But in the distance, on a crumbling stone monument, I saw faint letters carved into the surface.

— Lumeria.

Perhaps that was the name of this dying planet.

“Wait for me, Lumeria. I will not force this planet to change. I will only help it begin circulating again by itself.”

A dry wind blew.

Within that wind, there was the faintest scent of water.

I began walking in that direction.

My name from my previous existence was Mini.

Now, I was a traveler with a human body.

In a dying otherworld, my journey to restore the breath of a nearly stopped planet had begun.
