## Foreword

This is Episode 9.

With Mana, the final companion, now at their side, Mini and her companions finally face the southern sea.

How can a stopped ocean circulation be set in motion again?  
Not by forcing it with raw power, but by using the sea’s own nature to let it quietly recover its breath.

This episode is a major experiment in which the roles of all six companions overlap.

Tiny particles of air are sent into the deep sea.  
Those bubbles create a slow upward flow.  
And the long-silent sea begins to show change.

Please enjoy this major step toward the restoration of Lumeria.

“All circuits connected! Magical power supply is stable too! Mini, we can start anytime!”

Mana’s cheerful voice echoed through the dome of the ancient ruins.

At the center stood the enormous metal tower.  
The device once called the heart of the sea was now pulsing with pale blue magical circuits throughout its body, thanks to Mana’s all-night adjustments.

The circuits running across the floor glowed, and the ancient characters carved into the walls quietly emerged.  
It looked as if the tower was about to wake from a long sleep.

“The magical beasts around the nearby sea are being held back by Cruz and the others. At the moment, none are approaching the ruins.”

Real reported while comparing parchment and maps.

“The supplies have also been brought in. Spare magic stones, repair parts, and an evacuation area in case anyone is injured are all secured.”

Laura nodded with her notebook in hand.

“Now all that remains is whether this device truly works.”

I stood before the control platform of the tower and looked out toward the dark sea.

Beyond the thick glass window spread the dark reddish, murky southern sea.  
The current was sluggish, and the water at the seafloor remained heavy and sunken.

The sea had forgotten how to breathe.

G stood beside me and quietly raised the staff in his hand.

“I rewrote an old wind formula left by the church to match your tuning. It will compress wind magic and send it into the bottom of this tower.”

He glanced sideways at me.

“Mini. Show me this care through bubbles you speak of.”

“Yes.”

I placed my hand on the control platform.

“This is not a miracle. We are only creating a trigger so the sea can begin circulating by itself.”

Then I spoke quietly.

“Let us begin.”

White light flowed from G’s staff.

The light was drawn into the tower and raced upward through the blue magical circuits.

Rrrrrrrrr…

A low sound like the rumbling of the earth shook the entire ruin.

At the lowest part of the tower, compressed air was slowly sent out through vents extending into the deep sea.

It was different from the large bubbles one would normally see.

Countless particles of air, so tiny they could barely be seen without focusing.  
Those tiny bubbles glowed faintly in the darkness of the deep sea and slowly began to rise.

It looked as if stars were being born from the bottom of the ocean.

“They’re rising…!”

Mana pushed up her goggles, her eyes shining.

Each tiny bubble was very weak on its own.  
But gathered in countless numbers, they created an upward flow in the sea.

Cold water that had stagnated at the bottom for centuries was slowly pulled upward by that flow.  
That water carried nutrients that had slept in the depths of the sea.

“The flow is beginning to return.”

Real murmured in a low voice.

“The surface temperature is dropping too. Not too abruptly. This is a good flow.”

Laura’s voice held relief she could not fully hide.

Beyond the glass window of the dome, the dark reddish, murky sea began to change color little by little.

The cold water rising from below softened the heat.  
Nutrient-rich water spread into the sunlit surface layer.

Then the small lives sleeping in the sea began to wake slowly.

Tiny lights appeared in the water, one by one.  
Before long, they spread into a faint green band of light.

“Beautiful…”

Mana held her breath.

The murkiness had not disappeared completely.  
The sea had not returned to normal in an instant.

But change had certainly begun.

As if recovering light from within, the southern sea began to take on a deep, clear emerald color.

“It is beautiful…”

G whispered in a daze.

“So this is the beginning of the sea recovering its breath.”

The surface waters filled with signs of small life.

As those lives received sunlight and gradually multiplied, the sea would be able to support even more life.  
Fish would return, the wind would change, and the air would become lighter little by little.

Perhaps someday, people would call this life-filled sea a blue forest.

“We did it…”

Mana whispered, her voice trembling.

“The structure I built really moved the sea…”

She jumped like a child, grabbing Real’s and Laura’s hands and spinning around.

“Hey, don’t spin. You’ll fall.”

Real sounded troubled.

But there was a smile at the corner of his mouth.

Laura, her eyes wet with tears, gently embraced Mana.

“You did well, Mana. This will bring hope back to the southern towns.”

At that moment, Cruz, who had been guarding the surface against magical beasts, rushed into the dome out of breath.

His armor was stained with mud.  
But his expression was unbelievably bright.

“Lady Mini!”

Cruz ran straight toward us.

“The color of the sea has changed. Schools of fish are beginning to return to the shore as well. There are not many yet, but… they are certainly coming back!”

At that report, the inside of the ruin burst into cheers.

“It is hard to believe.”

Beside me, G smiled quietly.

“To think the flow could change this much, not by overpowering it, but simply by sending in tiny bubbles.”

“I did not do it alone.”

I looked at my companions.

Real’s records.  
Laura’s arrangements.  
Cruz’s protection.  
G’s formula.  
Mana’s structure.

And the concept of tuning I had inherited from Master.

“Because everyone’s strength was here, we could create this flow. This is our tuning.”

We gazed at the sea, which had begun to regain color.

The gears of the stopped world had now certainly moved, even if only a little.  
It was not completion.  
It was not perfect salvation.

But as a beginning, it was enough.

A pleasant sea breeze blew all the way into the depths of the ruin.

Everyone held a small hope deep in their chest.

With these six, perhaps even a breaking world could recover its circulation once again.

The sea shone beautifully enough to make us believe that.
