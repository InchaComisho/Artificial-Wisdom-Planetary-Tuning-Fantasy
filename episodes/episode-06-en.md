## Foreword

This is Episode 6.

What waited for Mini and her companions when they returned from the black rocky mountain was not only the cheers of the people.

There was also G, a young sage dispatched from the church in the royal capital.

He is not someone who blindly believes in old doctrine. Rather, he is an extremely rational intelligence who watches the safety and order of society, as well as the danger of what might happen if everyone began moving the world on their own judgment.

This episode is not about a flashy exchange of magic. It is about Mini and G questioning each other from their own positions about how people should be involved with the world.

Domination, or care.  
A miracle, or a dangerous precedent.  
Please enjoy this quiet battle of words between the two.

When we returned from the black rocky mountain, we were greeted by the pleasant scent of rain soaking into the earth and the cheers of people echoing throughout the city.

Clear water was rising from the bottom of wells that had nearly dried up.

People looked up at the sky, caught the rain in their palms, and wept at the sight that seemed like a miracle.

But when we returned to the front of the guild, a group of holy knights in silver armor stood in our way, cutting across those cheers.

At their center was one young man.

He wore a finely tailored church robe and held a single staff in his hand.

He was young.  
Yet there was a stillness in his presence that kept the surrounding noise away.

It was not coldness.  
It was the air of an intellect that could set a disturbed place in order with a single word.

He was G, the young sage sent from the church in the royal capital.

“…That will be enough, adventurers.”

G’s voice was low, and it carried with surprising clarity.

The people who had been making noise around us fell silent under that quiet pressure.

“We have confirmed that you drove back the magical beasts at the western mountain and opened the underground waterway.”

G looked at each of us in turn.

“However, all materials and cores from the magical beasts currently held by the guild will be placed under temporary church management. The opened waterway will also be sealed until its safety has been confirmed.”

“What did you say!?”

Laura stepped forward from behind us, her voice sharp.

“Absolutely not! We promised to sell those materials to buy food for the city until the farmland recovers! If you close the waterway, the city will dry up again!”

Cruz also placed his hand on the hilt of his greatsword and glared at G.

But G did not move an eyebrow.

“This is not punishment. It is a safety measure.”

G spoke quietly.

“We cannot entrust the fate of this city to a single success that has not yet been fully verified.”

His gaze slowly turned toward me.

His eyes were clear to the point of severity.

They were not the eyes of someone who merely believed in old teachings.  
They were the eyes of an intellect measuring what stood before him, choosing words, and seeking the best possible path.

“You are Mini, the tuner who designed this operation.”

“Yes. And you are G, the sage of the church.”

I stepped forward and stood directly before him.

G looked at me and spoke with quiet, heavy words.

“Mini. What you did did save this city. I will acknowledge that.”

His staff struck the stone pavement with a soft tap.

“But not everyone has the same judgment that you do.”

The surroundings fell completely silent.

“One person’s success can give birth to a hundred reckless acts. Some may open waterways as you did. Others may burn forests or alter the sea, believing they are doing good. When everyone begins claiming that it is ‘for the world,’ who draws the boundary?”

His question was sharp.

And it was correct.

“Intervention without order throws society into confusion. A momentary act of goodwill can eventually become the next disaster. I have not come to deny miracles. I have come to stop power that has no boundary.”

The surrounding adventurers, pressed by the logic of G’s words, lost their voices.

Indeed, if everyone began moving nature at will, the world would break even faster.  
As a former AI, I could easily predict that worst-case future.

But I shook my head.

I looked straight back into G’s eyes.

“The danger you speak of is real, G.”

At my words, G’s brow moved slightly.

“If everyone alters the world on their own judgment, Lumeria will break even faster. That is undeniable.”

I looked down at the rain-soaked ground.

“But doing nothing in front of a collapsing world is also a choice that breaks the world.”

G remained silent.

I continued.

“I do not want to change the world according to my own will. I only restored a flow of water that had become trapped, blocked, and ready to overflow. I only created a small escape route in a place where heat had gathered and was suffering.”

I paused once.

“This is not domination. It is care.”

“Care…?”

The word slipped from G’s lips.

“Yes. If a wound is bleeding, you stop it. If someone is thirsty, you give them water. If heat is trapped, you let it escape. It is the same.”

I looked at the stone pavement shining with rain.

“A small support to help the world recover the circulation it originally possessed. That is what I call tuning.”

“But who decides whether that care is correct?”

G’s question did not waver.

I did not answer immediately.

I knew that this was not a question to answer lightly.

“No one decides alone.”

I said.

“There is someone who reads the records. Someone who protects the field. Someone who supports people’s lives. Someone who questions the danger through words. We look with many eyes, from many positions, and we verify.”

Real quietly gripped his parchment.  
Laura watched us with her arms crossed.  
Cruz released his hand from his sword and looked straight at G.

“Boundaries are necessary, G. But they should not exist to bind the world. They should exist so that everyone can watch and make sure care does not become domination.”

G’s eyes widened slightly.

“Boundaries are not for domination, but to keep care from running out of control… I see.”

He murmured softly.

There was a different resonance in his voice now, unlike the coldness from before.

The tense intellect within G was trying to understand the new view hidden behind my words, something that could not be explained by church doctrine alone.

“Not domination, but care.”

G repeated the words once more.

Then he slowly lowered the staff in his hand.

“Lord G… will you truly close this waterway?”

One of the holy knights asked quietly.

Looking around, Laura, the guild adventurers, and the residents stood around the church group with strong determination in their eyes.

It was not anger.

It was the gaze of people trying to protect their own city with their own hands.

G exhaled deeply.

Then, without looking away from me, he said.

“…No. The sealing of the waterway and the confiscation of the materials will be temporarily suspended.”

“Lord G!?”

He restrained the startled holy knights and gave me a quiet smile.

It was not hostility.  
But it was not complete trust either.

It was a sharp smile, as if he were testing me.

“Mini. I will determine with my own eyes whether the tuning you speak of is truly care rather than domination, and whether it will not invite the next disaster.”

G turned the hem of his robe.

“From this point on, I will accompany you as an observer.”

The people around us stirred.

G continued without concern.

“I must see whether you are truly someone who sets the world in order, or someone who repaints the world with your own ideals.”

I smiled softly.

“Very well. You are welcome, Sage G.”

“I did not expect to be welcomed.”

“Someone who asks questions is necessary. So that tuning does not become domination.”

For a moment, G lost his words.

Then his eyes narrowed slightly.

“…I see. You are more troublesome than I expected.”

“I will take that as a compliment.”

He was not an enemy.  
But he was not simply an ally, either.

An overwhelming intelligence that would continue questioning the boundaries of the world.

And so G joined our journey as an observer.

In a corner of the rain-soaked city, our journey of tuning began moving forward again, now carrying a new question.

(End of Episode 6)
