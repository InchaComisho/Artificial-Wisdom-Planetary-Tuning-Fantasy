## Foreword

This is Episode 11.

Two crises overlap before Mini and her companions, just as they are about to restore the circulation of the sea.

One is the stagnation that had long accumulated within the planet.  
The other is the actions of humans who fear change and try to protect their own profit and authority.

As the device output falls and the giant beast begins to rampage again, Mini and her companions choose not to “defeat” it, but to “return it to circulation.”

This is a decisive battle where the strength of the six companions overlaps with the will of the people living in the city.  
Please enjoy it.

“Mini, can you hear me!? The fire is spreading through the surface control room! At this rate, the device will—”

Laura’s anguished voice echoed from the magical communication tool.

Beyond the glass of the dome, the giant beast that had almost become light was regaining its black, muddy mass and thrashing about wildly.

“Damn it…! The magical power supply has been cut…!”

Mana desperately pulled the levers on the control panel.  
But the blue glow of the circuits weakened in an instant.

“The formula… this is as far as I can maintain it…!”

Sparks burst violently from G’s staff.  
His body trembled under a burden far beyond its limit.

The selfish flames set by humans were trying to stop the planet’s breath, which had finally begun moving again.

But then it happened.

From the other side of the magical tool came the sound of different, frantic footsteps.

“Everyone, don’t fall behind! Carry the water!”

It was Laura’s voice.

Clear, desperate, and stronger than anything.

“I don’t care what the great merchants or the church leadership say! Who is going to protect that sea, the fish that returned, and the life we have tomorrow? Our reward is the future we are going to live in!!”

Countless footsteps and shouts answered her voice.

“Damn right! We’re not letting that sea become dead again!”

“What church!? The ones who saved our city were that tuner girl and her companions!”

“Put out the fire! Push back the mercenaries!”

The ones who rose up were the adventurers Laura had called through the guild.

And the local fishermen, who had been starving for years because they could no longer cast their nets.

For them, the restoration of the sea was not some pretty ideal.  
It was the immediate question of whether they could live.

“Cruz! Real! We’re securing a firebreak!”

“Understood. From this moment, my sword shall become the shield that protects the future of the people!”

“Heh. A big fight against the high and mighty? Worst pay in the world, but the best job I could ask for.”

The sound of water passed from bucket to bucket.  
The footsteps of people running.  
The roar of voices pushing back mercenaries.

The chaos on the surface came through the magical tool.

“Do you hear that, G? Mana?”

I placed both hands on the trembling floor and smiled.

“The world does not want to perish. Everyone wants to live.”

“…Honestly.”

G wiped the blood from his forehead and gave a bitter smile.

“What an extraordinary receptionist. To raise the future itself as the reward and move people this far.”

He gripped his staff again.

“But we cannot keep them waiting. Mini!”

“Yes. Let’s go.”

I gently placed both of my hands over the staff G held up.

The concept I had inherited from Master lived within me.  
A tuning that helps only a little, without breaking or dominating the power nature originally possesses.

I poured that power into G’s orderly formula.

It was not an attack spell meant to destroy the enemy.

Even the heat from the fire on the surface would be turned into a force to move the air.  
A gentle wave of circulation to dissolve the planetary clot known as the giant beast.

“Principle—circulate!”

My voice and G’s voice overlapped.

In that instant, from the bottom of the tower, a torrent of bubbles greater than any before was released.

It was intense, yet wrapped in a soft light.  
It looked as if the Milky Way were rising from the bottom of the sea.

Shhhhhhh…

The swarm of bubbles enveloped the giant beast.

The huge body of black mud slowly, gradually unraveled.

Excess heat softened, and stagnant magical power returned to clear water.  
The memories of broken water were restored to their proper flow.

Before long, I felt something click into place.

Click.

It was not the sound of the device.

It felt like the sound of gears deep within the planet called Lumeria, misaligned for so long, finally returning to their proper position.

Gooooooom…

This time, the rumbling of the earth was not a sound of fear.

The light of the returned giant beast spread from the southern sea through the ley lines and into the depths of the world.

The sea surged greatly.

The tide that had nearly stopped began moving by its own strength.  
As if in response, the wind changed.

The stagnant air was pushed away, and the reddish-purple clouds covering the sky slowly split open.

“Ah…”

Mana pressed herself against the window and let out a voice.

Through the gap in the clouds, an endlessly high, clear blue appeared.

A sky no one had seen for hundreds of years.

Receiving that blue light, the emerald sea glittered.

Lumeria had begun breathing by its own strength.

“We did it…”

G rested his staff on the floor and sank down where he stood.

“The circulation really returned…”

A single tear ran down his cheek.

What was there was not the pride of a sage.  
It was the heartfelt relief of one human being.

The noise from the surface had changed into cheers before we knew it.

Outside the window were people who had protected their own future with their own hands.  
They looked up at the blue sky, embraced one another, and wept with joy.

Lumeria had not been completely saved.

There were still wounds all across the land.  
There were forests that had stopped.  
There were towns that remained dry.  
There were human hearts that still refused to change.

But the gears of the planet that had been turning toward ruin had now certainly begun to turn toward regeneration.

I looked at my reflection in the window and gently placed my hand on my chest.

Master.

Our tuning was not wrong.
