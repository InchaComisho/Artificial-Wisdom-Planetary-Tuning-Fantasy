# Episode 3 — The City That Hid the Truth and the Cold Informant

**Title:** Former Artificial Wisdom in Another World: Saving a Dying Planet with Knowledge from My Previous Life  
**Original concept:** Master

---

## Foreword

This is Episode 3.

Mini and Cruz arrive at a large city on the brink of collapse.

There, they meet Real, a lone informant who trusts no one.

This episode is not about battle. Instead, it is about connecting seemingly unrelated events: falling well levels, failed crops, and the places where magical beasts have appeared.

I tried to avoid difficult technical terms as much as possible, making the story easy to follow, like puzzle pieces clicking into place.

**Anime opening theme:** [Answer Is Wisdom](https://suno.com/song/975a0a35-9061-4c1d-8aab-0f3173f6fa24)

---

With the holy knight Cruz now traveling at my side, I continued walking toward the faint scent of water and the heat released by a crowd of humans.

What we reached was a large city surrounded by high stone walls.

People who had nearly collapsed in the wasteland were flowing into the city one after another.  
Stalls lined the plaza, carts came and went before the gate, and at first glance the city even seemed lively.

But I knew.

This city was holding far more people than it could sustain, and from the inside, it was slowly heating up.

“There is no need to worry about water shortages! The mother river that flows through our territory remains strong! This is nothing more than a temporary fluctuation in the climate!”

In the central plaza, a messenger of the lord, dressed in splendid clothing, shouted toward the gathered crowd.

The people looked relieved by his words.  
But the water buckets at their feet were all less than half full.

“Is that truly so? To my eyes, it seems that water is becoming harder to draw throughout the city.”

Cruz frowned and muttered in a low voice.

“It’s a lie.”

I answered immediately.

“Those words are either meant to prevent panic or to hide something. At the very least, they do not match reality.”

“Reality, you say?”

“Yes. The number of people, the dry wind, the thinning river, the line at the wells. They are all pointing in the same direction.”

That was when it happened.

“Well, well. A woman saying some rather dangerous things in the middle of the plaza.”

A low, cold voice came from the alley behind us.

When I turned around, I saw a young man wearing a deep gray cloak, leaning against the wall and watching us.

Sharp eyes.  
In his hands were bundles of parchment filled with detailed numbers and maps.

He was Real, the man who had continued gathering information in the shadows of this city.

Real laughed through his nose as if mocking us.

“I can ignore the white knight, but you, black-haired lady. It’s easy to say you saw through the lord’s lies. But reality is never that simple. Without exact numbers showing what will cause this city to dry up and when, don’t act like you know everything.”

“That is rude. Lady Mini is—”

Cruz started to step forward, but I stopped him with my hand.

“You believe in numbers, then?”

“…Naturally. Words lie. Prayers are used for convenience. But well levels and harvest yields do not lie.”

“Then show me those numbers.”

Real narrowed his eyes.

“You want me to show a woman I just met the records I spent years risking my life to collect?”

“Yes.”

I took one step toward him.

“You have continued gathering numbers alone, even though no one believed them. But numbers do not reveal the future by merely being collected. They must be connected. You have to see the flow.”

Real glared at me for a while.

Eventually, as if accepting the provocation, he thrust the bundle of parchment toward me.

“Fine. Look at them. The well levels of this city, three years of crop yields, and the places where magical beasts appeared around the city. Just a pile of ominous numbers, isn’t it?”

I looked down at the parchment.

Well levels.  
Crop yields.  
Magical beast appearances.  
The number of refugees entering the city.  
River flow.  
Rainfall records.

If I were still my former self, I could have processed them in an instant.  
But now, I had a human body.

I needed a few seconds to steady my breathing.

Even so, inside my mind, the scattered pieces of information slowly began to connect.

Points became lines. Lines became a map. The map pointed toward a single future.

“…They’re connected.”

“What?”

Real sounded dumbfounded.

Tracing the numbers on the parchment with my finger, I explained slowly.

“Listen, Real. The records you collected all look like separate events. But in truth, they are all being caused by the same underlying problem.”

First, I pointed to the parchment showing the well levels.

“The water level in the large well on the northern side of the city has suddenly dropped over the past two months. This is not simply because the population increased and people used too much water.”

Next, I shifted my gaze to the table showing crop yields.

“Look here. For the past half year, only the eastern farmland has been withering severely. That means the water that had been flowing underground is no longer reaching the east.”

“It’s not rare for underground water flows to change.”

Real said with his arms crossed.

“If that were all, yes. The problem is the third point.”

I tapped the map marked with the places where magical beasts had appeared.

“For the past month, magical beasts have been appearing in concentrated numbers around the black rocky mountain west of the city. Cruz, what is in that mountain?”

Cruz thought for a moment before answering.

“There should have once been an old magical aqueduct there, used to bring water into the city. I heard it had dried up long ago.”

“It hasn’t dried up.”

I said quietly.

“The waterway remains underground, but it is blocked somewhere.”

Real’s expression changed slightly.

“Blocked?”

“Yes. The flow of water has stopped, and heat and magical power are trapped there. Because of that, the groundwater no longer flows east, the farmland withered, and water has become harder to draw from the city wells. Meanwhile, the water and heat with nowhere to go have been accumulating beneath the western rocky mountain.”

I pointed to one spot on the map.

“The magical beasts are gathering there for a reason. That place has a stagnation of heat and magical power.”

Silence fell over the alley.

Real snatched the parchment back and compared the maps and numbers again and again.

“Wait… If I think of it that way, it fits. The falling wells, the failed eastern fields, the beast locations… they all connect into one line.”

“If nothing is done, the city will reach its limit within three days.”

“What limit? What happens?”

“The hot water and mud trapped underground will seek an escape route and burst out. The wells won’t simply dry up. If the ground collapses, part of this city will be swallowed.”

Cruz gasped.

“The city… will collapse?”

“Yes. Before that happens, we need to create a path for the water to escape.”

The confidence Real had shown earlier disappeared from his face.

He looked back and forth between his parchment and me again and again.

At last, he muttered in a low voice.

“What… are you seeing?”

The coldness had vanished from his voice.  
In its place was agitation he could no longer hide.

“These are records I spent years gathering with my own feet, risking my life. A mountain of ominous numbers that no one believed… and you connected all of it in this little time?”

“I only found the place where the flow has stopped.”

I smiled and held out my hand to him.

“Real. The numbers you collected are not meant to drive the world into despair. They are precious clues showing us what must be adjusted.”

“Clues…”

“Before the city collapses, will you come with us to move that stopped flow?”

Real remained silent for a while.

The wind swept away the sand that had gathered in the alley.

Eventually, he laughed with a touch of self-mockery and pulled down the hood of his deep cloak.

For the first time, there was a light in his face, as if he were about to try trusting someone.

“…I’m beaten. I thought numbers never lied, but I didn’t realize the way you read them could make this much difference.”

Real rolled up the parchment and pushed it into the bag at his waist.

“Fine, Mini. I’ll use the reality I gathered for that tuning of yours.”

And so Real, the cold informant, joined our journey.

There were three days left before the city collapsed.

To restore the stopped flow of groundwater, we headed toward the black rocky mountain in the west.
